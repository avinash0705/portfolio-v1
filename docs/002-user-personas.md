# User Personas

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Active

---

## Purpose

`001-vision.md` defines two audiences: primary (technical decision-makers) and secondary (engineering peers). This document breaks the primary audience into the two distinct roles that actually visit — a hiring/engineering manager and a technical recruiter — because they read the portfolio differently and need different things from it in different amounts of time, and adds the secondary audience as a third persona.

Exactly three personas are defined. No others should be added without revisiting this document.

---

## 1. Engineering Manager / Hiring Manager (Primary)

**Who they are**
A senior engineer, engineering manager, or technical founder with hiring authority or strong influence over a hiring decision. They have already been technically screened themselves and can personally judge engineering substance, not just presentation.

**Why they came**
A recruiter, referral, or application forwarded them this portfolio as part of evaluating the author for a specific role. They are deciding whether to move the author forward — to an interview, a technical conversation, or an offer.

**Questions they're trying to answer**
- Can this person actually solve hard engineering problems, or do they just know the vocabulary?
- How do they think through trade-offs when there is no single right answer?
- Would I trust them with ownership of a system that matters?
- Is there anything here that would change how I run their interview?

**Time available**
5–10 minutes on a first pass. If something holds their attention, they will go deeper into one or two artifacts (usually a case study) for another 10–15 minutes.

**Content that matters most**
Case studies with real decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes. Evidence of ownership at production scale. Anything that reveals judgment under ambiguity, not just technical execution.

**Desired outcome before leaving**
They form a specific, defensible opinion — not a vague positive impression — and either request a conversation or come into the interview process already knowing what they want to probe further.

---

## 2. Technical Recruiter (Secondary)

**Who they are**
An in-house or agency recruiter with working technical vocabulary but limited ability to independently judge engineering depth. They are often the first filter before a hiring manager ever sees the candidate.

**Why they came**
Sourcing candidates for an open role, or vetting an inbound application, and need to decide whether to pass the author forward to the hiring manager.

**Questions they're trying to answer**
- Does this person's background plausibly match the role I'm hiring for?
- Is there enough here to justify forwarding them?
- What would I say to the hiring manager to get them interested?

**Time available**
1–3 minutes. This is a scan, not a read.

**Content that matters most**
A fast, legible summary of role, scope, and impact — headline-level facts a non-specialist can restate accurately (title, company, scale, technologies, outcomes). A resume or resume-equivalent view they can screenshot or forward. They will not read a full case study.

**Desired outcome before leaving**
They can confidently forward the author to the hiring manager with a one-line reason why, without needing the author to explain anything first.

---

## 3. Fellow Engineer (Community)

**Who they are**
Another practicing engineer, encountered through shared writing rather than a hiring funnel — a journal post, a case study shared online, a conference talk, or a mutual connection.

**Why they came**
Curiosity about a specific technical problem or piece of writing, not evaluation of the author for a role. They may have arrived at a single page (e.g. one journal entry) rather than the homepage.

**Questions they're trying to answer**
- Did this person actually solve the problem the title promised?
- Is there something here I can learn, reuse, or disagree with?
- Is this author worth following or engaging with further?

**Time available**
Variable — if the specific piece of content is relevant to them, they will read the whole thing (10+ minutes); if not, they leave in under a minute.

**Content that matters most**
Depth and honesty in the specific artifact they landed on — technical detail, lessons learned, and willingness to discuss trade-offs or failures, not just wins.

**Desired outcome before leaving**
They leave with something of value (an idea, a technique, a lesson) and, ideally, follow, share, or return for future writing — extending the portfolio's reach back toward the primary audience.

---

## What This Document Is Not

This document does not discuss layout, navigation, visual design, or implementation. It defines who the portfolio must serve and what each of them needs — later documents (information architecture, content strategy, page specs) must be built to satisfy these three personas, not the reverse.
